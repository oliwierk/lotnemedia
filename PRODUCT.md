# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Polish businesses, institutions, cultural organizations, and media professionals evaluating a video/photo production studio. They arrive mid-funnel: already aware they need production work, comparing vendors, and deciding whether to trust this team before reaching out. Decision-makers and marketing leads, often on desktop. First impression matters enormously.

## Product Purpose

Marketing and portfolio site for Ilona Ptak / Lotne Media, a Katowice-based media production studio. Services: TV reports (TVP), podcasts, aerial drone footage, event coverage, photography. Primary conversion goal: visitor reaches out via the contact form or email. Secondary: establish authority and credibility through the work itself.

## Brand Personality

Elegancja, Lekkość, Ruch. The name "Lotne" means airborne or fleeting. The brand is editorial cinema: precision journalism meets kinematic beauty. Confident but not corporate. The quality of the work speaks; the interface doesn't shout.

## Anti-references

- Generic agency "corporate stock-photo" look: sterile grids, inauthentically polished, interchangeable with any studio.
- Event agency style: loud palettes, aggressive CTAs, conference-poster layouts, rainbow gradient boxes.
- Current palette (cream / near-black) is intentional and should be preserved.

## Design Principles

1. **The image is the message.** Visual work is the hero. Every layout decision exists to serve the footage and photography, not to decorate around it.
2. **Earned space.** Whitespace is a craft decision, not a default. Dense sections should feel intentional, open sections should feel deliberate.
3. **Motion implies quality.** Movement is central to the brand. Animations reinforce the idea that Lotne Media captures things in motion; they are never decorative filler.
4. **Press confidence.** Editorial hierarchy over marketing noise. Headlines lead; copy supports. Nothing begs for attention.
5. **Show, don't tell.** Let work, numbers, and client names carry the authority claim. Avoid superlatives in copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.0 AA minimum (already partially addressed). Keyboard navigability, sufficient contrast ratios, screen-reader labels on forms and decorative elements.
